History of Brazil

Brazil’s history begins long before European contact, with millions of Indigenous people from diverse nations such as the Tupi, Guarani, and Yanomami living across the land for thousands of years. In 1500, Portuguese explorer Pedro Álvares Cabral arrived and claimed the territory for Portugal, marking the start of centuries of colonial rule. The Portuguese exploited natural resources—especially brazilwood (which inspired the country’s name), sugarcane, and later gold—using large-scale enslaved African labor, making Brazil one of the major destinations of the Atlantic slave trade.

By the early 1800s, when Napoleon invaded Portugal, the Portuguese royal family fled to Brazil, making Rio de Janeiro the capital of the Portuguese Empire. This elevated Brazil’s status and paved the way for independence. In 1822, Dom Pedro I declared Brazil independent, becoming the first emperor of the Empire of Brazil, a unique monarchy in the Americas. The empire lasted until 1889, when the monarchy was overthrown and Brazil became a republic.

Throughout the 20th century, Brazil experienced cycles of democracy, authoritarianism, and economic change, including a military dictatorship from 1964 to 1985. Since the return to civilian rule, Brazil has become one of the world’s largest democracies and an important global economy. Today, it is known for its cultural diversity, Amazon rainforest, sports achievements (especially football), and vibrant society shaped by Indigenous, African, and European roots.
